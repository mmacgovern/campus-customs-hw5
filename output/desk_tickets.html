My first impressions

What surprised me

I was honestly impressed by how fast the runs were and how well the agents worked together. Ticket 101 went from the Boss reading the ticket to a final resolution in 58 seconds (3:56:53 to 3:57:51 PM). Ticket 102 took 50 seconds and ticket 103 64 seconds. In that time the team made 38, 21 and 28 MCP tool calls. What really got me was seeing the Boss send two hand-offs in the same second: on ticket 101, Boss → Inventory and Boss → Accounting both went out at 3:56:54. Then Inventory turned around and handed its findings to Accounting at depth 2. It felt like watching a real team split up the work.

What frustrated me

Two things. First, the board stamped all three tickets RESOLVED, but in data/campus_customs_new.db ticket 101 is still in_progress and ticket 103 is waiting_approval. Only 102 says resolved. The board uses its own rule (run finished + nothing pending), while the database holds whatever the Boss last wrote with update_ticket_status. Two different "truths" is exactly what you don't want on an operations desk. Second, the token bill: 75,419 tokens for ticket 101, 48,040 for 102 and 63,434 for 103, which is 186,893 tokens in total. That's a lot for one tee, one rent payment and one quote.

Which agent impressed me, and which disappointed me

The same agent did both: the Boss. It impressed me because it never sent a specialist that had no business on a ticket. Facilities stayed out of 101 and 103, and Inventory stayed out of 102. On ticket 102 it even noticed mid-run that I had approved request #3, and asked Customer Service for a corrected draft (#3, "The rent payment has been executed") instead of leaving the wrong one (#2, "pending human approval") as the latest. But it disappointed me on ticket 103. I rejected PO #4 ($264.00 to Bulldog Print Co) at 3:59:52 PM, and 21 seconds later the Boss still wrote "PO approval #4 is pending" in its final note and set the ticket to waiting_approval. It never re-checked list_approvals before closing out.

What I'd change

I'd cut the redundancy so the whole process is more efficient. The audit trail shows a lot of repeated work. On ticket 101 alone:

  • get_ticket was called 7 times (Boss 2, Accounting 3, Inventory 1, Customer Service 1).
  • get_cash_balance was called 5 times and list_approvals 6 times.
  • Accounting was handed work 3 separate times.

My fixes would be:

  • One hand-off per specialist. Write that rule into boss.md.
  • Put the facts in the delegation task (SKU, invoice id, amounts) so teammates don't re-read the ticket.
  • No Customer Service for rent notices.
  • A required final check: the Boss calls list_approvals right before update_ticket_status.
  • Re-sync after each run: the backend re-checks a ticket's status when its run ends, so the board and database always agree.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102 is the clearest case. Facilities made exactly one tool call (get_rent_due_and_cash), and Accounting already has that same tool plus create_payment_request. A single "Accounting" agent could have handled lease 1's $2,400.00 rent to Elm City Properties in about 4 calls: get_ticket → get_rent_due_and_cash(1) → list_approvals → create_payment_request("rent", 1). Instead, 21 tool calls and 48,040 tokens went into hand-offs, re-reading the ticket, and two landlord drafts nobody needed.

Ticket 101's core was also a straight line: check CC-TEE-WHITE S stock → check Bulldog Print Co's hold → prepare the $840 payment for invoice 501 → (maybe) the $8 PO → draft to Tauhid. One agent with those five tools would have avoided the 7 get_ticket calls and Accounting being asked three times. It might even have spotted the "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S" double order, since it would have held all the facts in one head.

Where the team still earns its keep is separation of duties. Customer Service has no money tools. Accounting can only prepare, never pay. No agent can see execute_approved_payment. That's real protection when agents touch real customers and real money. My conclusion: simple, single-domain tickets like 102 should go to one agent, and the multi-agent team is worth its cost when a ticket crosses stock, money and customer communication, like 103.

5. Three new problems this team could NOT solve, and what I'd add

New problemWhy our tools can't do itNew tools and agents I'd need
The Yale AI Club pays for its hoodies, and the shop needs that money to fund the $264.00 restock. No revenue is modeled: cash only goes out. execute_approved_payment can only lower the balance, and get_cash_balance can only read it. No tool adds money to cash_accounts, so ticket 103 is stuck at $152.00. A Sales / Billing agent with create_customer_invoice, record_customer_payment (a human-approved deposit into checking), and a sales table. Then Accounting could approve PO #4 after the deposit lands.
Bulldog Print Co delivers the tee and hoodies, and the stock needs updating. execute_approved_payment moved $8.00 for PO #2 but didn't change inventory, so check_stock_and_pricing still shows CC-TEE-WHITE S = 0. Nothing tracks expected arrival (2026-09-05) or receipt. A purchase_orders table with expected arrival dates, a receive_shipment(po_id, qty) tool that raises inventory, and a Receiving agent (or an extended Inventory agent) that then asks Customer Service to tell Tauhid his tee is in.
Approving a discount or price override, like the $50/hoodie quote on ticket 103. The approval flow (create_payment_request, create_purchase_order_request) only covers payments and POs for CC-HOOD-NAVY and the like. So the Boss could set $50 by itself, and Customer Service's save_customer_draft put it straight into draft #4. There's no record of who approved the price. A create_price_override_request(sku, unit_price, qty) tool that uses calculate_margin and lands on the board's approval column, and a quotes table. Customer Service would only quote prices with an approved override. A Pricing agent could own this instead of the Boss.
If I did this again, I'd keep the guardrails exactly as they are (human approval, a pay tool that refuses negative balances, drafts that never send) and spend my effort on the redundancy fixes and these three missing tools.
